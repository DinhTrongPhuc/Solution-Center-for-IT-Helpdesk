# Escalation

When and how to escalate issues beyond Tier 1.

*Goal: To define clear risk-management protocols and support tiers to ensure complex issues reach the right experts efficiently.*

  

## **Section Overview**

Escalation is our operational safety net. This section provides the criteria for when to move a ticket to a higher tier and defines the specific roles responsible for each system.

  

## **Core Principles**

- **Risk Mitigation**: High-risk incidents (security breaches, outages) must be elevated immediately to prevent business disruption.

- **Knowledge Hierarchy**: Tickets should flow progressively from Tier 1 to Tier 3 only after initial diagnostic efforts are exhausted.

- **Ownership Retention**: Tier 1 agents maintain communication ownership even when a ticket is technically escalated to the Dev team.

- **Blameless Culture**: Individuals act in good faith based on the best available information. Escalation is a systemic improvement opportunity, not a personal failure.

  

## **Key Sub-sections**

- **[3.1 When to Escalate](3.-Escalation/3.1-When-to-Escalate)**: Check 4 criteria: security breach, system outage, repeated failure after 3 attempts, or beyond your system access. Escalate immediately if any applies.

- **[3.2 Tier Definitions & Routing](3.-Escalation/3.2-Tier-Definitions-&-Routing)**: Tier 1 handles diagnosis + workaround. Tier 2 (Senior) handles complex logic/config. Tier 3 (Dev) handles code fixes. Route only after exhausting current tier.

- **[3.3 Emergency Contacts](3.-Escalation/3.3-Emergency-Contacts)**: For system-wide failures, contact Engineering Lead first. For data fixes, contact Tech Team. Use role-based contacts — do not contact individuals who are off-shift.

*Note: Escalation is a strategic decision to ensure student success, not a transfer of responsibility.*
